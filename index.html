A question most parents ask themselves

Have you ever thought about sending your child to a private school?

Most of us have. And most of us looked at the fees — $30,000 to $40,000 a year — and moved on. Told ourselves it would be fine. But the question never fully went away.

Where this started — my story

I moved on.
But the guilt didn't.

I am a parent in South Auckland. I work hard. My wife works hard. We have a mortgage on a house we cannot sell because the market dropped. The school closest to us is not one anyone mentions when they talk about Auckland's best.

My daughter is 12. She comes home and says it matter-of-factly: "Our school is broke, man. We are broke." Not angry. Not sad. Just how things are. That lands harder than anything.

I am into Technology. I build systems for a living. And even I do not know what to do for my own daughter.

The guilt is not irrational. It is telling me something real. The difference between schools is not just the classroom — it is the map of opportunities that comes with the fees.

Nobody is showing me that map. Or how to build one myself. That is the gap I am fixing.

The spend problem nobody talks about
You are probably already spending.
The question is whether it is building anything.

Most Auckland families spend $2,000–$4,000 a year on activities. Theatre, soccer, music, tutoring. And two weeks every school holiday — four times a year — when busy parents scramble to find something for their child to do.

Holiday programmes alone add up to 8 weeks a year. Horse camps, music workshops, coding days, drama intensives. Good programmes. But chosen randomly, without a framework.

Private schools solve this automatically. The research is done. The structure is built. You are paying $35,000 for someone to have already figured this out. You should not have to.

Why random activities do not add up
Random activities don't compound.
Connected experiences do.

A child who writes a story → enters a competition → gets feedback → tries journalism. Those are not four separate activities. Each one strengthens the next. The connection is what builds capability.

Most parents are building disconnected. Not because they do not care. Because nobody showed them how the pieces fit together.

Connected intentional experiences build pathways. Private school students get this framework built for them. That is part of what the fees buy. It should not cost $35,000 to get it.
